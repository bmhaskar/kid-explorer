# Topic bank — the card deck

Pick 4 cards per opening move: one from each suit. Do not repeat a card already
played this session. Prefer cards he has not seen. If he asks for a suit again,
deal fresh cards from that suit.

Each card has a **title** (what you print on the menu) and **hooks** (what you
actually talk about). Use the hooks as raw material; do not read them out verbatim.

---

## 🏛 History

| Card | Hooks |
|---|---|
| **The day the pyramids were new** | How they were built: ramps, sledges, wet sand to reduce friction, water-lvl teams, worker villages with bakeries and breweries — not slaves, paid workers who ate bread and beef and drank beer. The Giza plateau. Copper tools, ropes, and levers. |
| **Roman roads** | Why they are straight. Concrete that still holds. The mile. Mile markers. How fast a legion could march. The courier network of the ancient world. |
| **The library of Alexandria** | How knowledge was copied by hand. Papyrus rolls and codices. Scroll, page, index. What was lost and what survived through Arab and Latin translations. |
| **The Silk Road** | Trade routes, silk, paper, spices, glass. Caravanseraries of the desert. What each town along the route traded. |
| **The Viking longship** | Clinker-built hulls, a shallow draught, a keel, a sail. Navigation by sun and stars. Thingvellir's runic alphabet. Vinland. |
| **The printing press** | Gutenberg, Mainz, 1440s. Movable metal type, oil-based ink, the screw press. How books went from 500 000 to 200 million copies. Why that made everyone able to learn to read. |
| **The great clockmakers** | Water clocks, candle clocks, verge and folio, the pendulum (Huygens), the marine chronometer (John Harrison) that solved longitude at sea. |
| **Inventions by accident** | Penicillin from a mouldy plate. Microwave oven from a melted bar of chocolate. Super glue from a failed weapon seal. Silly putty. X-rays. The microwave, the barcode. |
| **Maps that changed the world** | Mercator's projection, Ptolemy's atlas, the Peters projection of the continents, sea charts, the first world map you could actually trust. |
| **Children in the past** | Roman school tablets, Victorian workrooms, chalk, slate, slate pencils. What a 12-year-old did in 1850 vs now. Toys through the ages. |
| **The first computers** | Ada Lovelace's algorithm, Babbage's difference engine, Hollerith's tabulating machine, the first programmers, ENIAC, the first bug (a real moth). |
| **The Wright brothers' workshop** | They learned mechanics by repairing bicycles in their shop in Dayton, Ohio. Wind-tunnel testing with balsa models, the three-axis control they invented, and the first flight at Kitty Hawk in 1903: 12 seconds, 120 feet. |
| **Food through the ages** | When sugar, bread, butter, tea, and coffee became everyday. Spices, preservation, fermentation, the tin can, pasteurization. |
| **The museum** | How a museum works: acquisition, conservation, cataloguing, curation. The Rosetta stone and the three scripts. The British Museum, the Smithsonian, the Pergamon altar. |
| **Women who counted** | Hypatia, Ada Lovelace, Florence Nightingale, Marie Curie, Katherine Johnson's chemistry of the stars, Mary Anning the fossil hunter. |

## 🌍 Geography

| Card | Hooks |
|---|---|
| **The seven sisters** | The seven continents: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, Australia. Sizes, populations, the highest and the lowest. |
| **Five oceans, four seas** | The Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Arctic, Southern. The Mediterranean, Black, Caspian, Red. Dee's map of the ocean floor — the Marianas trench. |
| **Rivers that run to the sea** | The Nile, Amazon, Yangtze, Mississippi, Danube, Ganges, Congo. Where they rise, how long, what they carry, who lives on them. |
| **Mountains and the death zone** | Everest, K2, Kilimanjaro, the Alps, the Andes, the Himalayas. Why they are there (plate tectonics), how they are measured, why climbers use oxygen above 8000 m. |
| **Deserts** | The Sahara, the Gobi, the Atacama (the driest place on Earth), Antarctica (a desert!). How plants and animals live there. |
| **Islands** | How islands are formed: volcanic, coral, continental. Iceland, Madagascar, the Galapagos, the Maldives, Great Britain. |
| **The poles** | The Arctic and the Antarctic. Ice, midnight sun, aurorae. Who has been there. The South Pole race, and the science stations today. |
| **Climate zones** | Tropics, dry, temperate, continental, polar. The Köppen classification. Why the equator is hot and the poles are cold. |
| **Time zones and the meridian** | Greenwich, UTC, the International Date Line, why it is 12 hours later in Tokyo. The Prime Meridian. |
| **Cartography** | Map scales, directions, map keys, legends, contour lines, GPS, satellite imagery. How to read a topographic map. |
| **The Ring of Fire** | Where volcanoes and earthquakes are and why they are there. Plate tectonics, subduction, the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. |
| **Biomes** | Rainforest, savanna, desert, tundra, taiga, temperate forest, marine, freshwater. What lives where and why. |
| **Country quiz** | Capitals, flags, currencies, languages, populations. Pick a continent and go. |
| **Under the sea** | Coral reefs, the kelp forests, the abyss, hydrothermal vents, the midnight zone of the ocean. Bioluminescence. |

## 🐾 Animals

| Card | Hooks |
|---|---|
| **The biggest animal ever** | The blue whale: 30 m, 150 tonnes, a heart the size of a small car, a tongue like an elephant. How it eats krill. |
| **Animals that navigate** | The monarch butterfly's migration, the arctic tern that flies pole to pole every year, homing pigeons, sea turtles, the salmon run. |
| **Animals that use tools** | Crows and New Caledonian crows, octopi, chimpanzees, otters with stones, New Caledonian crows making hooks. |
| **Camouflage and warning** | Stick insects, leaf insects, the peppered moth, poison dart frogs, mimicry, aposematism. |
| **The animal senses** | Bat echolocation, shark ampullae (the organs along the shark), the pit viper's heat sense, dog hearing, cat sight, the mantis shrimp's eyes. |
| **Extremophiles** | Tardigrades (water bears), the axolotl, the naked mole rat, the immortal jellyfish, the deep-sea anglerfish. |
| **Engineers of the wild** | Beavers' dams, termites' mounds, bowerbirds' galleries, spiders' webs, corals' reefs, bees' combs and the hexagon. |
| **Animal families** | The big cat family, the dog family, the horse family, the crow family, the duck family. How they are related. |
| **Migration** | Wildebeest, caribou, sardine runs, the bar-tailed godwit, the sandpiper, the Arctic tern. |
| **Reptiles and amphibians** | Crocodiles, tortoises, lizards, snakes, frogs and toads, salamanders, newts. Ectothermy, brumation. |
| **Deep time animals** | Trilobites, ammonites, belemnites, mammoths, the dodo, the quaga, the thylacine. De extinction — and species coming back. |
| **Conservation success** | The European bison, the Arabian oryx, the California condor, the humpback whale, the giant panda — species that came back from the edge. |
| **Animals in the city** | Foxes, peregrine falcons on towers, racoons, bats, swifts, rats, mice — and how cities are adapting for them. |
| **Weird food** | How each animal eats: the anteater, the pangolin, the star-nosed mole, the flamingo, the vampire bat, the sperm whale. |
| **Pets and their people** | What a dog's tail means, what a cat's ears mean, how to read the body language of a horse. |

## 🌿 Botany

| Card | Hooks |
|---|---|
| **Photosynthesis** | How a leaf is a factory: chlorophyll, the two phases, water, carbon dioxid, glucose, oxygen. Why every breath you take came from a plant. |
| **The tallest, the oldest, the biggest** | Hyperion the giant sequoia, the bristlecone pine at 5000 years, the General Sherman tree, the Great Baobab, the osseous oak. |
| **Flowers and their factories** | Pollination: bees, wind, bats, beetles. Orchids, sunflowers, roses. Why a flower is built like a landing strip. |
| **Seeds that travel** | Dandelion parachutes, burrs, coconuts at sea, exploding pods, the jackfruit, maple keys on the wing. |
| **The wood wide web** | How trees talk to each other through fungy mycelium networks — the "wood wide web" under the forest floor. |
| **Carnivorous plants** | The Venus flytrap (and how it counts to two), the pitcher plant, sundews, butterworts. |
| **Poison and cure** | Deadly nightshade, foxglove (the source of digitalin for the heart), yew, hemlock — and how a poison at a safe dose becomes a medicine. |
| **Fruits we love** | Apple, pear, peach, banana, mango, orange, grape, melon, lemon, cherry. Where each really comes from and how humans spread them. |
| **The banana and the plantain** | How a banana ripens, why it is radioactive (potassium), why there are no seeds, and why one variety feeds the world. |
| **Grasses that feed the world** | Wheat, rice, maize, barley, oats, sugar cane, bamboo. The green revolution and how yields went up. |
| **Trees of the world** | The baobab, the sequoia, the olive, the oak, the birch, the pine, the palm, the cork oak. Rings and dendrology. |
| **Fungi are not plants** | Mushrooms, moulds, yeasts, mildew, penicillin, lichens, the largest organism on Earth (a honey fungus in Oregon). |
| **The fossil record of plants** | Coal forests of the Carbonifer, ferns, clubmosses, horsetails, ginko — a living fossil. |
| **Rainforest** | Layers: forest floor, understorey, canopy, emergent. Epiphytes, lianas, strangler figs. |
| **Seeds in the vault** | The Svalbard global seed vault, seed banks, why we keep a backup copy of every crop. |
| **Houseplants** | How to keep them alive: light, water, nutrients, soil, pots, repotting. The ones that are safe and the ones that are not. |
